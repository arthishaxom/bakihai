BakiHai — architecture explainer

It's a notebook, not a whiteboard

Same week of dinners, eggs and UPI payments. Two ways to build the app. One of them survives the metro with no signal.

1. The same dinner, stored two ways

You pay ₹900 for dinner for you, Rohan and Priya. Watch what the app actually stores.

Normal app — CRUD

Stores the current state

bills(id: 1, what: "Dinner", amount: 900, settled: false)

When Priya pays you back, the app overwrites the row:

UPDATE bills SET settled = true -- the old value is gone forever

“Who marked this settled, and when?” — the app no longer knows. If you want to know, you build an audit log as a second system.

BakiHai — event log

Stores what happened

#a1 You paid ₹900 "Dinner" split 3 #a2 Rohan borrowed 3 eggs from You #a3 Rohan returned 1 egg to You #a4 Priya paid ₹300 to You (UPI) #a5 You confirmed #a4 ✓

Lines are never changed. A new fact is a new line. Your balance screen is just arithmetic over the whole notebook.

Want to know who settled what, when? It's line #a5, forever.

Why "append-only"? Because that's how ledgers have worked since before computers: you don't erase an entry, you add a correction entry. Git, bank statements, accounting books — all append-only. The rules feel unusual only because most apps quietly mutate data and lose the trail.

2. The metro test — where CRUD breaks and the notebook doesn't

Both phones are offline in the metro. Both people act on the same dinner bill. Then everyone reconnects.

You (offline)

Tap: “Priya settled her ₹300”
intent: settled = true

Rohan (offline)

Fixes the bill: “we also ordered chicken, ₹1050 total”
intent: amount = 1050
CRUD on reconnect You save → settled: true, amount: 900 Rohan saves → settled: false, amount: 1050 …one of these silently overwrites the other.

This is called last-write-wins. Someone's money quietly disappears. To fix it properly you need locks, conflict dialogs, or a server that serialises every edit — i.e. you start building a distributed database to avoid losing data.

Notebook on reconnect #a6 You confirmed Priya paid ₹300 #a7 Rohan corrected dinner total to ₹1050

Two new lines. Nothing is overwritten, so there is nothing to resolve. The balance recalculates from the whole notebook and both facts are respected. Merge conflict code: zero.

Two people can't conflict on a line that doesn't exist yet — it's a group chat, not a shared Google Doc.

3. How one line travels to your friends

Your phone never talks directly to their phone. Neither does it ask a server for the balance. It drops a sealed envelope on a noticeboard.

What the noticeboard can see

That 6 envelopes arrived, their timing and size, and the room's name. It's a postman counting letters.

timingsizecount
What it can never see

Amounts, names, items, notes or who paid — the contents are sealed with your group's key, which only group members' phones hold.

₹ amountspeopleeggsnotes
The relay is a mailbox, not a database. It never computes balances, never runs rules, never “owns” your data. If it dies, every phone still has the complete notebook and sync resumes later. Phone lost? It redownloads all envelopes and rebuilds everything. This is also why there is no login: your device has a key, an invite link carries the group's secret, and that's the whole account system.

4. Why this is genuinely better for this app

Offline is the default

The app reads and writes your phone's copy. No loading spinners, no “can't reach server”, works in the metro, on a train, on flight mode.

Restore = replay

A new phone rebuilds the whole book by replaying envelopes. Backup isn't a special feature — it's the architecture.

Privacy by construction

Even the host (Cloudflare) can't read your money life. This isn't a privacy policy promise; it's math.

No login, no accounts DB

Invite link + device stamp. The most bug-prone, most phished part of a normal app simply doesn't exist here.

Costs nothing, scales with friends

Free relay tier covers a group like yours by orders of magnitude. No server bill, no “free trial” cliff.

New features are cheap

A new feature is a new line type. Old app versions ignore unknown lines instead of crashing — like adding a new emoji to a chat.

5. The honest costs — and why each one exists

Cost 01 — Compaction

A notebook that only gains lines gets heavy. At ~10,000 lines it's still only a few megabytes, but the sync engine also keeps small traces of removed lines. Why: that's the price of never losing history. Fix: every ~2,000 lines we fold old ones into a “closing balance” checkpoint, exactly like accounting closes a month. One-time feature, then automatic.

Cost 02 — Metadata is visible

The relay sees that envelopes moved, and when. Why: routing letters requires seeing the envelopes. Fix: contents stay sealed; only timing/size/count leak. Acceptable for a friend group, documented in the ADR.

Cost 03 — A removed member keeps old history

If someone leaves (or is removed), they already downloaded earlier envelopes and can still read those. Why: you can't un-send a letter someone already read. Fix: rotate the group key so all future lines are unreadable to them. True eviction means re-encrypting all history — overkill for friends, and we'll say so in the ADR.

Cost 04 — No accounts means link-holders can write

Anyone who has the group link can push junk lines. Encryption protects secrecy, not availability. Why: we traded accounts for zero-login. Fix: a long random secret in the link, signed lines, size/rate caps at the relay.

Cost 05 — More upfront work than CRUD

The sync layer is real work. Why we still choose it: the alternative isn't free either — a normal app still needs an API, auth, a database, backups, and still breaks in the metro. We pay once, upfront; CRUD pays forever, in infrastructure and lost edits.

Cost 06 — Balance is computed, not stored

Every balance is arithmetic over the lines. Why: a computed balance can't drift out of sync with the facts. Scale check: even 10,000 lines replays in a few milliseconds on a phone. Non-issue for a friend group.
